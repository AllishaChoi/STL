Tactical Patterns

01 / 04
01 — Fork
Knight Fork

One piece attacks two enemy pieces simultaneously. The knight on d5 forks King on b8 and Rook on f8 — opponent must lose material.

02 — Pin
Absolute Pin

Bishop on a8 creates a diagonal ray. The knight on d5 cannot move — doing so would expose the king on g2 to check. Pinned piece loses all offensive value.

03 — Skewer
Skewer

Inverse of the pin. Rook on a5 attacks King on c5 — king is forced to move, exposing the queen on e5 to capture. The more valuable piece is attacked first.

04 — Discovery
Discovered Attack

Bishop on d4 moves to g7 — simultaneously threatening a target while unmasking the rook's file. Two threats created in a single move.

05 — Back Rank
Back Rank Mate

Pawns on f7, g7, h7 cage the king on g8. Rook swings to e8 — checkmate. Own pawns become the prison. Advance one pawn to create an escape square.

06 — Zwischenzug
Zwischenzug

An "in-between move" — instead of the expected recapture, play a surprise check first. Forces opponent to respond, then recapture with tempo gained.

Endgame Technique

03 / 04
01 — KQ vs K
Queen + King vs King

Drive the lone king to any edge, then a corner. Use the queen to cut off escape squares while the king supports from a distance. Beware stalemate traps.

02 — Pawn End
Pawn Opposition

When kings face each other with one square between them, the player who does NOT have the move holds the opposition and can push the enemy king back.

03 — Lucena
Lucena Position

The fundamental winning rook endgame. Build a bridge — use the rook to shield the king from checks while the pawn promotes. The most important endgame pattern.

04 — Philidor
Philidor Defence

Black's rook on the 6th rank forms a wall. When the pawn advances, the rook shifts to the back rank delivering endless checks. The essential drawing technique.

Core Principles

04 / 04
01

Control the center with pawns or pieces. The squares e4, d4, e5, d5 are the battlefield. Whoever dominates the center dictates the course of the game.

02

Develop all pieces in the opening. Knights before bishops, avoid moving the same piece twice. Castle early to safeguard the king and connect rooks.

03

In the endgame, activate the king. The king becomes a fighting piece once most material is traded. Centralize it aggressively — opposition determines who wins pawn races.

04

Never make a move without a reason. Each move should improve your position, create a threat, or prevent an enemy plan. Purposeless moves are the root of most losses.
